## Identity, lifetime and fallback

An actual AI launch to a human receiver sets a pending capture only during live recording of canonical operation8. A later human launch within the same dispatcher clears that pending capture. The completed dispatcher captures the complete state, post-operation cursor, schema2/simulation3, live match epoch and physical receiving end. Cursor zero cannot identify a completed launch. The preview stream ABI still names the pre-operation cursor, so a qualified hit converts postcursor to postcursor minus1 with unsigned64-bit borrow.

A hit requires current incoming ordinalFFFE, matching receiving end/version/epoch, selection equal to the frozen latest history cursor and a retained nonzero origin at or before selection. Human launch, incoming terminal contact flags, non-playing lifecycle, a receiver becoming AI, retention expiry, attach/init/select/title reset or cursor lifetime exhaustion retire validity. Private seek/preview execution cannot capture or invalidate this live metadata. Freeze/resume preserve an eligible origin; historical seek does not silently replace it with a historical origin. No cache hit extends the retained cursor interval.

A miss uses the unchanged exact cold resolver. A historical request can legitimately end in missing context when no preceding opponent launch remains. Cache misses, schema/epoch mismatch, unsupported origins and missing context must not be relabeled as warm performance.
