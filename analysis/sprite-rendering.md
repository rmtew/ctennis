# Sprite composition and Amiga rendering candidate

The ROM uploads ten four-byte SG sprite records from `$C010-$C037` to VDP sprite-attribute VRAM `$3B00-$3B27`; `$D0` at `$3B28` terminates the list. Each record is Y, X, pattern number, colour. The source builds the **next** buffer after uploading the previous one, so the port must preserve that one-update presentation boundary.

The builder at `$10E9-$114D` writes three 16-by-16 records for each player: lower player in records 1-3, upper in records 5-7. It selects pattern numbers and offsets from fourteen five-byte descriptors at `$115A-$119F`. The ball builder at `$13B9-$1401` hides records 0, 4 and 8, then fills exactly one with the ball's sprite Y/X, pattern and colour. Its selected slot depends on ball court Y relative to the players, changing the source sprite-list priority of the ball. Record 9 shares RAM `$C034-$C037` with ball court-plane Y/X, pattern 0 and colour 1; this is the small ball shadow/court-position dot, rather than a spare sprite slot. In the controlled gameplay VRAM capture at frame 1310, sprite pattern 0 at `$1800` is `$C0,$C0` followed by zero bytes, a 2-by-2 pixel mark. A retained frame-600 screenshot shows the expected 2-by-2 black mark at the captured court coordinate.

Thus the source construction permits at most **eight intentionally non-hidden records**: six player pieces, one ball and one shadow. `python scripts/check_sprite_hardware_fit.py` checks 51 retained MAME RAM snapshots, 60 timing snapshots, and 201 serve snapshots. The simple active-screen filter (Y<`$C0`, nonzero colour) finds 36 with six records, 89 with seven, and 187 with eight. The observed maximum is four overlapping 16-line spans per scanline. These are bounded samples, not a proof of every mode and trajectory. In a controlled frame-1310 MAME capture, VDP sprite attributes `$3B00-$3B27` exactly equal RAM `$C010-$C037`; the full 16 KB VRAM SHA-256 is `9e86e6103f17060bd5f7649cfdd96d23047f1f422152e6a57fe28d1fcf84bb4f`.

Eight Amiga hardware sprite channels can carry those eight records without blitter redraws of the static court in the sampled states. The observed colours are SG indices 1, 4, 13 and 15. Compressing populated source slots in source order into Amiga channels 0-7 produces at most two distinct colours in each channel pair, within the three opaque colours available to a pair. Both systems give lower-numbered sprites higher priority, so this ordering preserves sprite-over-sprite priority. Conversion would turn each selected SG 16-by-16 one-bit pattern into an Amiga 16-pixel sprite image and build per-frame control words. Retain the source update/presentation boundary and keep simulation independent of PAL rendering.

The Amiga pair-colour and fixed-priority rules are documented in the [Amiga Hardware Reference Manual, sprite chapter](https://www.ikod.se/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Amiga_Hardware_Reference_Manual_3rd_Edition.pdf). Unseen mode-specific palette changes could still require per-frame remapping; a small ball or shadow remains a blitter fallback if a later constraint fails.

## Native Copperline prototype

`python scripts/run_amiga_sprite_probe.py` converts the controlled frame-1310 VRAM capture into four 256-by-192 bitplanes and eight Amiga sprite streams, assembles [sprite_probe.s](../amiga/sprite_probe.s), and launches it through Copperline on the exact PAL A500, OCS, 512 KB chip, Kickstart 1.3 profile. The current binary is 28,444 bytes, SHA-256 `ad581cd72b76e5ccc7d77a32ad4a675da416d99607b615be0a54cfe13a27d15b`. It uses chip-memory data, a Copper display list, four background planes, all eight sprite DMA channels, and paired sprite colours. The static court is reconstructed from captured VDP name, pattern and colour tables; the source screenshot agrees with those background pixels at 48,587 of 49,152 positions, with sprite overlays accounting for the bulk of the rest.

The [10-second GIF](../build/amiga/sprite-probe/gameplay-sprites.gif) begins at emulator second 4 and includes a 334 ms left-joystick hold at second 5. The probe starts with source lower-player X `$C0` and tick `$A9`, samples native port-2 joystick input, and advances simulation with a 59.922738 Hz fixed-point clock independent of the 50 Hz PAL display. Its lower-player movement uses the source's alternating two- and one-pixel steps and positions three hardware sprite pieces. Copperline debug records for 40 consecutive updates match the source MAME checkpoint tuples (normalized input, lower X, tick before write, held-update count and update ordinal); the first release has X `$A2`. The same comparison now includes the attached-ball Y/X and serve-vector step. Clock records show 59 simulation updates after 50 PAL frames and 119 after 100 PAL frames. The lower player, ball and shadow DMA positions use the previous simulation state, matching the source sprite-buffer upload boundary; this is an implementation of the observed serve-wait slice, not a general ball engine. The runner enforces that comparison against the retained source TSV. The [PNG](../build/amiga/sprite-probe/gameplay-sprites.png) shows the final court and players. Exact visual parity still needs aligned pixel comparison and palette calibration. Assets, executable, captures and report stay ignored under `build/`.

The first native capture exposed two setup errors: an incorrect sprite X origin pushed the lower player off-screen, and `BPLCON2=0` placed moving sprites behind the bitplane court. The current probe uses the corrected position origin and `BPLCON2=$0024`; both players now appear over the court. This is a bounded movement slice, not complete gameplay: serve trigger/launch, active flight, scoring, audio, mode transitions, full state checkpoints and long-run cadence remain to port or measure. The probe disables guest interrupts and runs until Copperline's bounded capture completes, so it is not yet an OS-clean game executable.

## Dynamic live sprite bridge

The [live integration probe](native-gameplay-integration.md) now scans all ten source records from the prior update, packs the active ones into eight DMA channels in source order, copies rows from a converted 64-pattern atlas, and patches the four sprite-pair palettes. The atlas is derived from the frame-1310 sprite-pattern VRAM; the hardware-fit checker verifies every pattern observed in the 312 snapshots against the converted rows. The snapshots use source ball slots 0 and 4 and SG colours 1, 4, 13 and 15. Live logs in the current left-movement capture show ball slot 4 and no bridge fit errors; a live serve is still needed to observe its slot transition on Amiga. Sprite pattern VRAM changes during play have not been established or supported by this bridge.
